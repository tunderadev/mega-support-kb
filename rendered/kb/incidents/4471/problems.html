10 of 12 subject lines show up under more than one problem.

Shipping the parser fix will not close #4471. The other two problems have different causes and need their own fixes.

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first

Every tenant in #4471 writes dates day first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so 8 June comes back as 6 August. This is the problem the incident page describes, and the queued fix is for it. It reaches every way in except the booking-v3 form: the app, email, and the booking-v2 form, whose hint already says DD/MM/YYYY.

Ticket #1.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type the month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what it says. The form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first, and the booking lands with day and month swapped. The symptom looks exactly like parser, but fixing the parser won’t change it. The hint is wrong, so the hint has to change: it should follow the tenant’s locale.

Only reports that came in through the form, at a tenant on booking-v3, belong here.

Ticket #2.

one-day: a date that moves by one day

These reports describe no swap. “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” The date shifts by a day, and it shifts differently for people in different places. That reads like a time zone bug, a date stored as midnight in one zone and shown in another. Day order has nothing to do with it, so neither fix above moves these back.

The desk already spotted them (desk-notes.json), but the incident page counts them under parser and puts them in a footnote. They get their own ticket here.

Ticket #3.

Telling them apart

  1. Read what moved. Day and month swapped means parser or hint. One day off means one-day.
  2. For a swap, check how the report came in. Through the form at a booking-v3 tenant is hint. Anything else is parser.
  3. Ignore the intake tag. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser, not the tenant, and the table shows how often it guesses wrong.
  4. Don’t merge on subject. The same subject lines turn up under different problems.
